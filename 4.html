Gram-Positive Bacilli / Spore-Forming — Bacillus & Clostridium species, built for fast review and active recall.

The essentials, not the whole lecture

Every rule and grouping worth memorizing before you touch a diagram or a quiz.

RULE 1 — Bacillus = aerobic, Clostridium = anaerobic. Both are Gram-positive, spore-forming, environmentally hardy rods.
RULE 2 — Spores let both genera survive for years in soil, dust, and water — infection happens when the spore finds a host.
Aerobe

Bacillus anthracis

  • Obligate endospore-forming pathogen; grows aerobically or anaerobically.
  • Differs from B. cereus by virulence plasmids.
  • 3 disease forms: cutaneous (most common, skin wound), pulmonary (inhaled spores), GI (ingestion, rare in humans).
  • Toxin = 3 proteins: PA (entry channel), EF (+PA → edema toxin), LF (+PA → lethal toxin).
  • Anthrax is primarily a disease of herbivores; humans infected accidentally.
Aerobe

Bacillus cereus

  • Normal soil inhabitant; causes gastroenteritis and ocular infections.
  • Heat-stable enterotoxin → emetic form (vomiting), classic with reheated rice, onset 1–6 h — true intoxication, not infection.
  • Heat-labile enterotoxin → diarrheal form, onset 8–16 h, acts on small intestine.
  • Ocular infection follows eye contact with soil-contaminated objects.
Anaerobe

Clostridium botulinum

  • Foodborne botulism from improperly canned/preserved/fermented foods.
  • 7 toxin serotypes (A–G); A, B, E, F cause human illness — type E linked to fish.
  • Toxin absorbed from gut → blood → binds presynaptic motor neuron receptors, blocking acetylcholine release → flaccid paralysis.
Anaerobe

Clostridium tetani

  • Spores in soil, animal/human feces, rusty tools; resistant to heat and antiseptics.
  • Toxin = tetanospasmin — blocks release of inhibitory neurotransmitters → continuous excitatory stimulation → rigidity and spasm.
  • Especially serious in newborns and pregnant women.
Anaerobe

Clostridium perfringens

  • Capsulated, non-motile; causes gas gangrene and food poisoning.
  • Alpha toxin — lyses RBCs/platelets/leukocytes, hemolysis, tissue and hepatic/myocardial damage; also drives the anaerobic environment in gas gangrene.
  • Beta toxin — necrotic lesions in enterocolitis.
  • Epsilon toxin — increases intestinal and vascular permeability.
  • Enterotoxin — heat-labile, causes colon food poisoning.
  • Spreading enzymes: collagenase, hyaluronidase, DNase.
  • Gas gangrene mainly driven by alpha + theta toxins together.

Four exam laws to lock in

  1. PA is non-toxic alone — toxicity only appears once combined: PA+EF = edema, PA+LF = lethal.
  2. Heat-stable = vomiting/emetic; heat-labile = diarrhea. Same organism (B. cereus), opposite toxin behavior.
  3. Botulinum blocks an excitatory transmitter (ACh) → flaccid paralysis. Tetanospasmin blocks an inhibitory transmitter → rigid paralysis. Opposite mechanisms, opposite presentations.
  4. C. perfringens toxin lettering (alpha/beta/epsilon) maps to distinct jobs: alpha = hemolysis, beta = necrotic enterocolitis, epsilon = permeability.

Anthrax toxin assembly

tap EF or LF to combine with PA
PA + EF = Edema toxin. PA binds specific cell receptors and, after proteolytic activation, forms a membrane channel that lets EF enter the cell. The resulting edema toxin is responsible for cell and tissue edema.
PA + LF = Lethal toxin. Same PA channel, different cargo. Lethal toxin is the major virulence factor and the principal cause of death in infected animals and humans.

Clostridium perfringens toxin map

tap each toxin
Alpha toxin+
Lyses RBCs, platelets, and leukocytes. Increases vascular permeability with massive hemolysis and bleeding, tissue destruction, hepatic toxicity, and myocardial dysfunction. Also drives the anaerobic environment central to gas gangrene.
Beta toxin+
Responsible for the necrotic lesions seen in enterocolitis.
Epsilon toxin+
Increases both intestinal and vascular permeability.
Enterotoxin+
Heat-labile toxin produced in the colon; responsible for C. perfringens food poisoning.
Spreading enzymes+
Collagenase, hyaluronidase (breaks down hyaluronic acid, raising tissue permeability to fluids), and DNase help the infection spread through soft tissue.
Gas gangrene: alpha + theta+
Alpha toxin's hemolytic activity cuts blood flow, keeping neutrophils, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils from reaching the site — producing the anaerobic environment the bacteria need. Theta toxin adds vascular degradation and triggers a platelet-activation-driven inflammatory response that constricts nearby arteries, reinforcing that same anaerobic environment.
